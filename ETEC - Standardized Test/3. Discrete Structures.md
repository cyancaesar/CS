# SKU 3.1

**Basic Logic, Sets, Relations, and Functions**

Topics:

1. Functions, relations, and sets.
2. Operations of sets, functions, and relations.
3. Propositional and predicate logic.
4. Informal logical statements and their conversion to predicate logic expressions.
5. Methods of symbolic propositional and predicate logic.
6. Proofs in propositional and predicate logic using inference rules.

SLOs:

3.1.1: Explain functions, relations, and sets with examples.
3.1.2: Perform the operations of sets, functions, and relations.
3.1.3: Describe propositional and predicate logic.
3.1.4: Convert informal logical statements to predicate logic expressions.
3.1.5: Apply methods of symbolic propositional and predicate logic
3.1.6: Construct proofs in propositional and predicate logic using inference
rules.
3.1.7: Use symbolic logic to model real-life situations.

# SKU 3.2

**Proof Techniques and Basics of Counting**

Topics:

1. Proof techniques and their structures (direct proof, proof by contradiction, and induction).
2. Relationship between weak and strong induction.
3. Well-ordering principle and its relationship to mathematical induction.
4. Counting arguments, including sum and product rules, inclusion- exclusion principle and arithmetic/geometric progressions.
5. Pigeonhole principle.
6. Permutations and combinations of a set.
7. Recurrence relations.
8. Modular arithmetic-based computations.

SLOs:

3.2.1: Identify the proof technique used in a given proof and its structure (direct proof, proof by contradiction, and induction).
3.2.2: Determine which type of proof is best for a given problem.
3.2.3: Explain the relationship between weak and strong induction with
examples
3.2.4: Discuss the well-ordering principle and its relationship to
mathematical induction.
3.2.5: Apply counting arguments, including sum and product rules,
inclusion-exclusion principle and arithmetic/geometric progressions.
3.2.6: Use the pigeonhole principle in a formal proof.
3.2.7: Compute permutations and combinations of a set in a real-world
application.
3.2.8: Solve different basic recurrence relations.
3.2.9: Perform modular arithmetic-based computations.

# SKU 3.3

**Graphs, Trees and Discrete Probability**

Topics:

1. Graph theory and types of a graph/tree.
2. Tree Traversal algorithms.
3. Spanning trees.
4. Isomorphism.
5. Probabilities of events.
6. Dependent and independent events.
7. Binomial distribution.
8. Bayes theorem and conditional probabilities.
9. Variance of a probability distribution.

SLOs:

3.3.1: Explain graph theory and each type of a graph/tree with examples.
3.3.2: Show various traversal methods for trees and graphs.
3.3.3: Model real-world problems in computer science using appropriate
forms of graphs and trees.
3.3.4: Describe how to construct a spanning tree of a graph.
3.3.5: Identify the isomorphism between two graphs.
3.3.6: Compute events’ probabilities for basic problems.
3.3.7: Identify the difference between dependent and independent events
with real world examples of dependent and independent events.
3.3.8: Compute a probability using binomial distribution with the variance.
3.3.9: Apply Bayes theorem to determine conditional probabilities in a
problem.
3.3.10: Solve problems using tools of probability.
